What Is a Toy Hauler?

An RV and a Garage.
At the Same Time.

RV + Garage = Toy Hauler
Your base camp and your rig, all in one

Your Campsite and Your Garage, Parked at the Same Spot

Here's the deal: a toy hauler is just an RV with a garage built into the back. That's the whole thing. You pull up to the campsite, drop the ramp door, roll out your ATV, and you're ready to ride. Then you come back, grill out, and sleep in an actual bed inside the same unit.

The garage isn't some little cubby either. We're talking 8, 10, 12 feet of dedicated floor space with tie-down rings, rubber flooring, and a ramp door that doubles as a deck when you're set up at camp. When the toys are out, that space becomes a bunkroom, a hangout, or just extra room to stretch out. It's an honestly great idea.

What Can You Haul?

So What Can You
Actually Haul?

Short answer: pretty much whatever you've got. Here's the real-world rundown of what our customers are loading up before they hit the Northwoods.

ATVs & Quads

Two full-size ATVs? Easy. We've had guys load a 700cc quad and still have room for their gear bags. If you're heading to the Chequamegon or the Northwoods ATV trails, this is how you do it right.

Motorcycles & Dirt Bikes

Your buddy's motorcycle that he swears he'll actually ride this time? Sure, throw it in. The D-ring tie-downs keep everything locked down tight, and the rubber flooring means you won't scratch anything up on the way to the track.

Side-by-Sides & UTVs

A Polaris RZR? Yep, the Vengeance Rogue has a garage wide enough. Can-Am Maverick? Same story. These are built for real machines, not just show. Check the garage width on the specific unit before you buy -- we'll walk you through that.

Kayaks & Canoes

Wisconsin's got no shortage of lakes and rivers to paddle, and a toy hauler garage is a surprisingly great place to store long boats. Stand them on end or lay them flat -- either way, they're safe, dry, and off the roof rack.

Bikes & E-Bikes

Mountain bikes, kids' bikes, e-bikes -- they all fit. If you're hitting the Kettle Moraine trails, throw the whole family's bikes in the garage and you're set. No rack, no straps on the outside, no drama at the trailhead.

Gear & Everything Else

Snowmobiles, generators, toolboxes, golf carts, hunting gear -- if it fits through the ramp door, it goes. We had a guy haul his whole ice fishing setup plus a sled last winter. If it's got wheels or it's heavy, this is how you move it.

Garage Features

The Garage Details
That Actually Matter

Not all toy hauler garages are the same. Here's what to look for -- and what every unit on our lot is equipped with.

01

Ramp Door / Patio Deck

The ramp door doubles as a patio deck. Fold it down, throw some chairs on it, and you've got the best tailgate setup at the campground. Zero-gravity models you can operate solo with one hand. It's one of those things that sounds like a gimmick until you've used it.

02

D-Ring Tie-Downs

Flush-mounted in the floor, recessed so you're not tripping on them. Run your ratchet straps through and your machines aren't going anywhere, whether you're on I-94 or a bumpy Northwoods two-track getting to the trailhead.

03

Onboard Fuel Station

Fill up your ATVs or dirt bikes right at the unit -- no jerry can juggling, no gas station run at 6am before you hit the trails. Separate tank from the main unit, properly vented, with a safety shutoff. It's one of those features you don't know you need until you've done a trip without it.

04

Separate Living Area

There's a real partition between the garage and your living space -- not just a curtain. The mud, the exhaust smell, the trail grime stays back there. You step through a door into an actual living area. This matters after a full day of riding.

05

Convertible Garage Beds

Unload the bikes, fold down the bunks, and suddenly you've got extra sleeping space for the kids, the brother-in-law, or whoever else tagged along. It's not fancy, but it works great, and it beats somebody sleeping in their truck.

06

Power & Lighting

12V and 110V outlets in the garage so you can charge batteries, run a work light, plug in your air compressor, whatever you need. Overhead LEDs make it a functional workspace, not just a dark hole. If you're doing any maintenance at the campsite, you'll want this.
