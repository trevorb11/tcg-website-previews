Understanding Fifth Wheels

What Is a Fifth Wheel RV?

The most spacious and stable towable RV on the market. More room, less sway, and a bedroom that's actually separated from your kitchen.

Premium Towable RV

The King of Towable RVs

A fifth wheel connects to a specialized hitch mounted in the bed of your pickup truck — the same kingpin coupling system that semi-trucks use. That's not a coincidence. It's what makes fifth wheels tow so much better than a bumper-pull.

Because the hitch sits right over the truck's rear axle, the weight distributes evenly and the trailer tracks cleanly behind you. Fifth wheels are more stable in wind than travel trailers — if you've ever white-knuckled a travel trailer on I-94 near Milwaukee, you know exactly what I'm talking about.

The raised front section gives you a bi-level floor plan, which means your bedroom is up front and actually private. You get more usable living space per foot than any other towable RV. These things are big enough to live in full-time — and a lot of our customers do exactly that.

Towing Guide

Towing Requirements — The Straight Story

I'd rather tell you the truth now than have you figure it out on the highway. Here's what you actually need.

You Need a Real Truck

You need a 3/4-ton truck. Yes, really. I know the internet says a half-ton can do it. The internet is wrong — or at least it's optimistic in a way that gets people in trouble. Towing at the edge of your vehicle's limits isn't smart, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise just to close a deal. Here's what you actually need to know.

  • 3/4-Ton Truck Is the Baseline — Ford F-250, Chevy Silverado 2500, Ram 2500, or equivalent. For most fifth wheels, this is the minimum for towing safely. A 1-ton (3500) gives you even more headroom. Don't cut corners here.
  • Fifth Wheel Hitch Required — It mounts in your truck bed. Not a ball hitch, not a weight distribution bar — an actual fifth wheel hitch. If you don't have one, we can point you to good installers in the area.
  • Check the Numbers Before You Buy — Compare your truck's rated towing capacity against the fifth wheel's GVWR (gross vehicle weight rating). Don't fudge this. I'll walk through the numbers with you — just bring your truck's spec sheet.
  • Payload Matters Too — People forget this. Your truck has to handle the pin weight (usually 15–25% of the trailer's weight) plus your passengers and whatever you're hauling. That payload sticker in your door jamb is the real number to watch.
  • Diesel Is Worth It on Heavy Loads — Gas engines can handle a lot of fifth wheels just fine. But if you're towing a big Chaparral over the Rockies, diesel torque and engine braking on the way down the mountain will make you glad you have it.
  • Lighter Options Do Exist — The Chaparral Lite and certain K-Z Spree floor plans are built with weight in mind. Some are towable by a well-equipped half-ton. We'll tell you honestly whether your truck qualifies — I'm not going to let you drive off with a unit that's going to give you trouble.
