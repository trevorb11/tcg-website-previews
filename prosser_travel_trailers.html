Why a Trailer Makes Sense

Why a Travel Trailer?

People ask me this a lot. Here's my honest take on why a travel trailer beats most other options for the average Wisconsin family who wants to get outside more.

Unhitch and Go Explore

This is the part people don't think about until after they buy a motorhome: once you're at camp, you're stuck. With a travel trailer, you pull in, unhitch, and your truck is free. Go into town, go fishing, drive the back roads — the trailer stays put. That flexibility alone is worth a lot.

Shop Travel Trailers

Use the Truck You Already Own

You don't need to buy a new vehicle to camp. If you've got a half-ton truck, you can tow a solid travel trailer right now. That's money that stays in your pocket instead of going toward a vehicle purchase you didn't need.

More Affordable Than You Think

No engine, no drivetrain, no motorhome-sized insurance bill. Travel trailers cost less to buy, less to insure, and less to maintain. You get more living space for your money than anything else in the RV world.

There's One for Your Situation

Whether you want something small enough for a RAV4 to pull or a full bunkhouse that sleeps eight, there's a travel trailer for it. The hard part is narrowing it down — which is honestly one of the more fun conversations I have with customers.

Maintenance Is Simple

There's no engine to blow up. Basic trailer maintenance — seals, brakes, bearings, keeping things clean — is stuff a lot of people handle themselves. When you do need service, it's a fraction of what a motorhome costs to fix.

Built for Real Families

Separate bedrooms, bunk rooms, outdoor kitchens, real bathrooms — today's trailers are nothing like what your parents camped in. If your idea of camping is sleeping on the ground, that's great. But if you want everyone to sleep well and have a good time, a trailer changes everything.

Wisconsin's Best Campgrounds Are Waiting

Peninsula State Park, Harrington Beach, Governor Dodge, Copper Falls — we've got incredible camping right here in Wisconsin. Most of our customers aren't driving to Yellowstone. They're spending the weekend two hours from home, and a travel trailer makes every one of those trips better.

Common Questions

Things People Ask Me All the Time

These are the questions I answer on the lot almost every day. I'll give you the same answer here that I'd give you in person.

What is the difference between a travel trailer and a motorhome?

The big one is that a travel trailer doesn't have its own engine — you pull it with your truck or SUV. That means lower cost to buy, lower cost to insure, and nothing to maintain under a hood. The other thing people underestimate is flexibility: when you get to camp, you unhitch and your truck is free to go wherever. A motorhome stays put. For most families, a travel trailer just makes more sense.

What size truck do I need to tow a travel trailer?

Look up your vehicle's tow rating in the owner's manual — that's your hard ceiling. Then stay meaningfully below it, not right at the edge. If you've got a Toyota Tacoma, don't let anyone sell you a 7,000 lb trailer. I've seen that happen and it's not safe. Stick under 4,500 lbs with a Tacoma and you'll be fine. Got an F-150? Depending on the package, you've got more room — maybe up to 8,000-9,000 lbs. An F-250 or F-350? You've got real options. Call me with your year, make, and model and I'll give you a straight number.

Do you offer financing on travel trailers?

Yeah, and we work with several banks — not just one. If the first one doesn't give you a rate worth taking, we try another. We'll walk you through exactly what you're signing and there won't be anything in the paperwork that surprises you. Just call and ask, we're happy to talk numbers before you even come in.

Can I trade in my current RV for a travel trailer?

Yes — bring it in. We've taken pop-ups, older travel trailers, fifth wheels, motorhomes. If it's got value, we'll put it toward what you're buying. It doesn't have to be perfect. The evaluation is free and there's no obligation. Worst case, you get a number and decide it's not the right time. That's fine too.

What travel trailer brands does Prosser's carry?

Right now we carry Coachmen Clipper, Coachmen Freedom Express Ultra Lite, Coachmen Cadet, Sunset Park (Retro, SunRay, Sun Lite), Forest River Viking, Gulf Stream Conquest, Dutchmen Aspen Trail, K-Z Sportsmen, and Jayco White Hawk. We've got new and used. Inventory changes, so if you're looking for something specific, just call or check the website — I'd rather you know what we've got before you make the drive.

What is the best travel trailer for a family of four?

Two kids under 12? I'd start with the Coachmen Freedom Express or the Jayco White Hawk — both give you a master bedroom up front, rear bunks for the kids, a real kitchen, and a bathroom. That's the layout that actually works for a family of four on a week-long trip. Come walk them in person, because floor plan photos don't tell you everything. You need to stand in the kitchen and imagine making breakfast.

Are lightweight travel trailers durable enough for frequent use?

For weekend and seasonal use — yes, absolutely. Aluminum framing and composite panels have come a long way and they hold up well. Where I'd pump the brakes is if you're thinking about full-timing in one. Most lightweight models aren't built for that. If you're camping 30+ weekends a year or going out for extended stretches, we should talk about models with better insulation and heavier construction. There's a right tool for every job.
