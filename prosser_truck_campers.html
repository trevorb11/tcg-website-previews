Understanding Truck Campers

What Is a Truck Camper?

New to truck campers? Here's the short version.

Slide-In Design

A Tiny Apartment That Slides Into Your Truck Bed

Think of it as a tiny apartment that slides into your truck bed. You keep your truck, you keep your parking spot, and you can camp literally anywhere you can park.

It mounts in the bed with tie-downs, has its own kitchen, sleeping area, and often a bathroom. When you're done camping, pop it off on its built-in jacks and your truck goes back to being a regular truck. Simple as that.

  • Slides into your pickup truck bed — no trailer needed
  • Kitchen, sleeping area, and often a full bathroom
  • Go anywhere your truck goes — forest roads, boat launches, two-tracks
  • Removable — take it off and your truck is back to normal
  • Fits in a regular parking spot — no special storage needed
The Truck Camper Advantage

Are Truck Campers Right for You?

Honest answer: they're not for everyone. Here's how to know if one is right for you.

You Want to Go Where Trailers Can't

If you're driving two-tracks in the Northwoods or backing down a rutted boat launch, you can't do that with a 30-foot trailer behind you. A truck camper goes wherever your truck goes — and that's the whole point.

You're Tired of Backing Up Trailers

No backing up. No sway bars. No worrying about the trailer swinging wide at a gas station. You drive it like a truck because it is a truck. That alone is worth it to a lot of people.

It's Just You and Your Partner

If you need space for 6 people, get a travel trailer. But if it's you and your partner and you want to hit every fishing spot in northern Wisconsin without worrying about where to park a big rig — this is it.

You Want to Camp Off-Grid

Onboard water, propane, and battery power mean you're not hunting for hookups. Pull off on a forest road, set up in five minutes, and you're home. No campground reservation required.

You Still Need Your Truck

Pop it off on its built-in jacks and your truck is back to being your everyday truck, your work truck, your hunting truck. You're not giving anything up — you're just adding options.

You Want to Keep It Simple

Lower price than a motorhome. Lower insurance. No separate tow vehicle. Fewer things to go wrong. A lot of our customers have owned bigger rigs before and just got tired of the complexity.

What's Inside

Truck Camper Features

You'd be surprised what fits in these things. Here's what's actually inside.

Real Kitchen

Two or three-burner stove, sink with running water, a fridge, and enough counter space to actually cook. Fry up walleye at the campsite. Make eggs in the morning. You're not eating granola bars out of a dry bag.

Wet Bath or Dry Bath

A lot of our customers ask about this first thing. Many models have a full bathroom — toilet and shower. Higher-end models separate the shower and the toilet so you're not crawling over each other. It matters on a week-long trip.

Comfortable Sleeping

Overcab sleeping area with a full or queen mattress. You're not sleeping on the ground and you're not crammed in a tent. Most couples sleep very comfortably in these.

Plenty of Storage

Overhead cabinets, under-dinette compartments, a wardrobe, and exterior storage for gear. Hunters especially like this — there's a real place to put everything, not just a pile in the corner.

Heat When You Need It

Forced-air furnace for cold nights. If you're hunting the Northwoods in November, you want heat that actually works. These have it. AC available too for summer trips.

Off-Grid Power

Deep-cycle battery, 12V and 110V systems, solar-ready on most models. Charge your phone, run the lights, keep the fridge cold. You don't need a campground to make this work.
