Direct Detection Technology

Why Direct Detection Changes Everything

Conventional electron microscopy cameras convert electrons to photons, then capture the photons. Every conversion step degrades the signal — losing information, adding noise, and fundamentally limiting what you can see.

Direct Electron's DDD technology eliminates the conversion. Our cameras detect image-forming electrons directly, using a radiation-hardened sensing layer where incident electrons leave an ionization trail that is collected and integrated to form a signal. No scintillator. No intermediary. Just direct measurement.

The result is dramatically better resolution, superior signal-to-noise ratio, and sensitivity that opens up experimental conditions — lower doses, faster acquisition, weaker signals — that were previously out of reach.

Applications

Enabling Discovery Across Disciplines

Direct Electron cameras are used by leading research groups across the full spectrum of electron microscopy applications.

Biological Cryo-EM

High-resolution structure determination of proteins, complexes, and membrane systems. Direct detection enables the low-dose imaging required to preserve beam-sensitive biological samples while achieving atomic or near-atomic resolution.

Materials Science TEM

Atomic-resolution imaging of materials, interfaces, defects, and nanoscale phenomena. Direct detection cameras provide the sensitivity and dynamic range needed to image a wide range of materials and phenomena at high resolution.

Low-Voltage Electron Microscopy

Direct detection's extended voltage range and photoblind sensors enable imaging at low accelerating voltages — opening up experiments that conventional cameras cannot support while reducing beam damage to sensitive specimens.

4D STEM

Scanning transmission electron microscopy with a full diffraction pattern collected at every probe position. Celeritas detectors enable 4D STEM at frame rates that make practical large-area 4D STEM acquisitions feasible for the first time.
