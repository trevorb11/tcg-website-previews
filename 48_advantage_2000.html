Our Mission

Fighting for the People Who Need It Most

Social Security Disability is not just a government program. For millions of Americans, it is the difference between keeping the lights on and losing everything when a health condition takes away their ability to work.

The Social Security Administration denies the majority of initial applications — even legitimate ones. The process is complex, the paperwork is overwhelming, and the hearings can feel impossibly intimidating without representation. That is why Advantage 2000 Consultants exists.

We have spent 30 years learning this system inside and out. We know what the SSA looks for, how to build a compelling case, and how to advocate effectively at every stage from application through appeal. You focus on your health. We fight for your benefits.

How We Help

From Application to Approval

We guide you through every step of the Social Security Disability process — from gathering your first medical records to representing you at a hearing if necessary.

1

Free Case Evaluation

Your first conversation with Advantage 2000 is completely free and carries no obligation. We review your situation, help you understand your options, and give you an honest assessment of your claim's strength. There are no promises we cannot keep and no fine print you need to worry about at this stage.

2

Application Preparation & Filing

A properly prepared application makes a significant difference. We help you document your condition accurately, gather the right records, and complete the SSA's paperwork in a way that gives your claim the strongest possible foundation from the start.

3

Gathering Medical Evidence

The SSA's decision depends heavily on your medical records and documentation. We work with your healthcare providers to obtain the right records, request necessary statements, and ensure your medical history tells the complete, accurate story of how your condition affects your ability to work.

4

Hearing Representation

If your claim is denied and reaches the hearing level, you should not face an administrative law judge alone. Our experienced representatives appear with you, present your evidence, question vocational and medical experts, and advocate clearly and confidently on your behalf.

5

Appeals — If Needed

A denial at the initial or reconsideration stage is not the end. The SSA's appeals process has multiple levels, and many claims that were denied early are ultimately approved on appeal. We evaluate every denial for appeal potential and pursue every viable path forward.

6

Benefits Secured

When your claim is approved, we help you understand your benefits, the onset date, and any back pay you may be entitled to receive. We celebrate your win and make sure you fully understand what you have earned. And remember — we only get paid when you do.

Who We Represent

We Are Here for You — Wherever You Are in the Process

First-Time Applicants

Starting the process for the first time can be overwhelming. We walk alongside you from the very beginning, helping you avoid the common mistakes that lead to unnecessary denials.

Those Who Have Been Denied

Most people are denied on their first application. A denial does not mean your claim is without merit — it often means it needed better preparation and representation. We can help you appeal.

People Waiting for a Decision

The SSA process can take months or even years. If your claim has been pending without resolution, we can review your case and help move things forward with additional documentation or follow-up.

Workers Injured on the Job

A workplace injury that has left you unable to return to your previous work may qualify you for Social Security Disability benefits in addition to any workers' compensation you receive.

People with Mental Health Conditions

Mental health conditions — depression, bipolar disorder, PTSD, schizophrenia — can be just as disabling as physical conditions. The SSA recognizes them, and so do we.

Veterans with Service-Related Disabilities

If you are a veteran with a service-connected disability that prevents you from working, you may be entitled to Social Security Disability benefits in addition to your VA benefits.
